# ADR-006: Scientific Provenance and Composition

## Status

Proposed

## Context

Laboratory Tasks may derive Materials from existing Materials through dilution, amplification, mixing, or other scientific transformations.

The inventory system must preserve these relationships so that a Material can be traced to its sources and downstream Materials can be identified when a source is found to be defective.

Scientific provenance is distinct from physical movement. A transfer between Containers may preserve Material identity, while a transformation may establish a new Material with its own scientific properties.

Composition also differs from quantity accounting. A preparation may contain contributions from several Materials without those contributions representing independently available inventory.

Preparations may accumulate contributions across multiple TaskExecutions before becoming final. Their intermediate composition must remain traceable, including when execution is partial or uncertain.

A source correction can make a downstream assertion stale through several levels of scientific dependency. Delayed, lost, duplicated, or out-of-order notifications, failed reassessment, and subsequently discovered dependencies make propagation alone insufficient to protect new authorizations.

We need explicit scientific derivation, composition, and validity dependencies without duplicating physical stock or relying on propagation Events for correctness.

## Decision

We represent **scientific provenance and composition as explicit, traceable Relationships and scientific assertions**, established through authorized Effects and independent of physical containment and quantity accounting.

Intermediate preparations retain their provenance and composition regardless of whether they become final outputs or are subsequently consumed by another Task.

### Scientific Provenance

When an output Material is derived from existing Materials, its provenance identifies the source Materials and generating `TaskExecution`, where applicable.

A derivation is represented conceptually as:

$$
\operatorname{DerivedFrom}(M_{\text{output}},M_{\text{source}},E)
$$

where \(E\) identifies the generating TaskExecution.

We adopt the following rules:

- Provenance is established through an authorized, validated Effect, not inferred from shared attributes, Container placement, or temporal proximity.
- Derivation Relationships preserve source and output Material identities, generating execution references, and supporting evidence.
- A Material may derive from multiple sources, and a source may contribute to multiple derived Materials.
- Provenance supports traversal in both directions, from a Material to its ancestors and from a source to its descendants.
- Material identity remains governed by ADR-001. Aliquoting may preserve identity, while scientific transformation may establish a distinct Material.

When a TaskExecution produces a new Material, the relevant provenance Relationships remain traceable alongside its accepted identity and quantity Effects.

Ordinary transfers preserving Material identity retain their physical lineage through MaterialMovements rather than creating redundant scientific derivation Relationships.

### Scientific Composition

For a composite Material, composition records its constituent Materials and their scientifically meaningful contributions:

$$
\operatorname{Composition}(M_C)=\{(M_i,r_i)\}_{i=1}^{n}
$$

Each contribution has an explicit measurement basis, such as proportion, concentration, or constituent quantity, according to the applicable `MaterialSpecification`.

Composition describes scientific makeup, not additional independently spendable inventory.

Inputs consumed during preparation are accounted for through MaterialMovements. The resulting composite Material has its own accountable quantity in its ContainerContents.

Output quantity is established through applicable measurements and evidence rather than universally equating it to the sum of constituent input quantities.

### Incremental Contributions and Intermediate Preparations

A composite preparation may accumulate contributions through multiple TaskExecutions before the intended final preparation is established.

We adopt the following rules:

- Intermediate preparations may have their own Material identity and ContainerContents, as established by ADR-001.
- Each confirmed contribution may establish an authorized Effect recording its source Material, generating TaskExecution, measurement basis, and supporting evidence.
- Accepted contribution Effects establish or extend the intermediate Material's provenance and composition.
- Contributions are associated with the corresponding quantity Effects without treating scientific contribution values as additional inventory balances.
- Accepted composition history is preserved as new contributions are established; existing assertions are not silently overwritten.
- Uncertain contributions are not accepted as confirmed scientific composition.
- Finalization validates the applicable composition and preparation requirements before establishing a final Material under `IdentityDecision`.

When finalization creates a distinct Material, its provenance references the intermediate Material. The intermediate's accountable quantity is consumed to the extent required by the accepted MaterialMovements.

Finalization does not erase the intermediate's identity, provenance, or historical contributions.

### Historical Integrity

Accepted provenance Relationships, scientific assertions, relevant decisions, and contributions retain their identities, governing Specification versions, generating TaskExecution references where applicable, supporting evidence, and required dependencies.

Corrections and supersessions establish new authoritative assertions or interpretations while preserving previous assertions, decisions, evidence, dependencies, and execution references. Previously accepted downstream conclusions are not automatically deleted or rewritten. Neither correction nor reassessment independently reverses physical activity, committed quantity Effects, or historical authorizations.

### Dependent Scientific Assertion Integrity

Accepted scientific assertions and relevant decisions explicitly identify the source assertion or decision versions or revisions used to establish their conclusions, supporting evidence, and governing Specification and policy versions. Applicable scientific contracts define the required dependencies, their intended-use and temporal scope, and which source changes require reassessment.

These dependencies support transitive validation and are not interchangeable with Material derivation Relationships. Scientific ancestry alone neither supplies every validity dependency nor makes every ancestor relevant to every use. Dependency records must be sufficiently complete to establish the requirements of the applicable contracts; missing required relationships or references cannot imply validity.

**Validity for a proposed use is established on demand by traversing the complete transitive graph of required dependencies against current authoritative state.** Validation distinguishes dependencies that are:

- Current and acceptable under the applicable contract.
- Authoritatively invalid for the intended use.
- Stale or requiring reassessment after a relevant correction or supersession.
- Unresolved because required relationships, information, or evidence cannot be established.

Current and acceptable does not universally mean referencing the newest assertion: the governing contract determines whether source validity matters at preparation, later use, or both, and whether a change requires reassessment. Any reaffirmed conclusion retains the source versions and evidence supporting it.

From commitment of a relevant correction, a downstream conclusion relying on the superseded basis cannot remain sufficient for new-use authorization while required reassessment is unresolved. ADR-007 applies this rule through on-demand eligibility evaluation and validation at the authoritative authorization boundary under ADR-011. An earlier assessment or accepted approval does not bypass that boundary.

A correction need not synchronously traverse or modify all downstream Materials, assertions, or decisions. Event-driven propagation, notifications, proactive refresh, and bulk descendant invalidation are not prerequisites for correctness. Authorization remains safe when those mechanisms fail or dependency relationships are later discovered or corrected; required dependency completeness and freshness must be established at evaluation, not inferred from prior Event processing.

Required traversal must terminate or yield an unresolved outcome. Cycles, malformed relationships, missing references, unavailable evidence, or inability to complete validation cannot establish authorization. No fixed provenance-depth limit is a scientific rule. Shared dependencies may be evaluated once within a consistent assessment; caching, incremental computation, and precomputed dependency information are permitted only when their completeness, freshness, and validity for that assessment can be established. Reassessment scheduling is independent of this validation contract.

### Initial Scope

The initial model supports explicit derivation Relationships, composition assertions, incremental contributions, versioned scientific dependencies, and on-demand transitive validation for eligibility. This does not require a generic dependency engine.

Automatic reassessment, downstream propagation, and proactive notification or refresh are deferred. Reassessment execution and scheduling remain separate concerns under ADR-007; deferring them does not defer validation or protection against stale authorization.

## Alternatives Considered

**Infer provenance from MaterialMovements.** Rejected because physical transfers do not necessarily establish new scientific identities, and quantity accounting does not fully describe scientific derivation.

**Represent composition as additional inventory balances.** Rejected because constituent contributions would duplicate available stock.

**Create a new Material for every aliquot.** Rejected because physical division does not necessarily establish a distinct scientific identity.

**Establish composition only when preparation is finalized.** Rejected because partial or interrupted preparations may contain confirmed contributions that must remain traceable.

**Automatically propagate all source properties to descendants.** Rejected because scientific dependencies and reassessment requirements are Specification-dependent.

**Rely on eager or event-driven downstream invalidation for correctness.** Rejected because incomplete propagation or later dependency changes must not permit stale authorization. Such processing may optimize responsiveness, but does not replace on-demand validation.

## Consequences

- Material origins and downstream derivations remain traceable.
- Intermediate preparations retain accepted contributions even when a TaskExecution fails or remains incomplete.
- Composite Materials can accumulate scientific contributions across multiple TaskExecutions.
- Final preparations retain provenance through their intermediate Materials without duplicating inventory balances.
- Scientific composition remains independent of physical quantity accounting.
- Uncertain contributions do not silently become accepted scientific facts.
- Provenance, composition, and contribution corrections require explicit validation and historical preservation.
- Source corrections requiring reassessment immediately prevent affected stale assertions from independently supporting new-use authorization.
- Authorization requires complete, current, applicable scientific dependencies, including transitive dependencies; incomplete validation cannot authorize use.
- Dependency validation has a cost that may be reduced only through demonstrably valid reuse of assessment work.
- Automated reassessment and propagation are optional for correctness; on-demand validation is not deferred.
