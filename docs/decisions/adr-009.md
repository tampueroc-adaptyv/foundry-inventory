# ADR-009: Events, Evidence, and Effect Commitment

## Status

Proposed

## Context

Laboratory Tasks produce observations, measurements, instrument responses, and reports of physical activity. These records may support changes to inventory, but they do not necessarily establish that those changes occurred or should be accepted.

For example, a robot may report a successful transfer without the corresponding MaterialMovement being committed. Conversely, a physical transfer may occur despite a missing acknowledgment.

The system must distinguish observed activity, proposed changes, and authoritative state transitions. This distinction is necessary to handle partial execution, uncertainty, retries, and recovery without inventing physical facts or losing accepted history.

## Decision

We distinguish **Events, supporting evidence, and Effects**, with authoritative state changes committed only by the responsible domain.

### Events and Evidence

An `Event` is an immutable record of an observed or reported occurrence.

Events retain their source, relevant subjects, occurrence time where known, recording time, and supporting evidence.

Events may originate from TaskExecutions, instruments, operators, or external systems. They may also exist independently of a TaskExecution.

Evidence may include measurements, barcode scans, instrument logs, human confirmations, certificates, and references to external artifacts.

Evidence is retained with sufficient provenance and integrity information to support historical interpretation.

An Event or evidence record does not independently establish an authoritative inventory change. Its sufficiency is evaluated under the applicable Specification and Task contract.

### Proposed and Committed Effects

An `Effect` represents a proposed or committed change to authoritative state.

Each proposed Effect identifies:

- Its stable logical identity and Effect family.
- The affected Entities and proposed change.
- Its originating TaskExecution or other authorized source.
- Applicable Specification versions.
- Required evidence and authorization.

A TaskSchema determines which Effect families a TaskExecution may propose and when.

The responsible domain independently validates the proposed Effect against current authoritative state, applicable Specifications, evidence, authorization, and domain invariants.

Only an accepted Effect changes authoritative state.

Committed Effects retain their identities and remain traceable to their origins and supporting evidence.

### Effect Commitment and Atomicity

Effects may be committed independently, as an atomic group, or according to explicit dependencies established by their governing contracts.

Atomic commitment applies only within a supported transaction boundary.

For example, all postings of one MaterialMovement commit together, while a TaskExecution may produce several independently committed MaterialMovements.

The system does not assume that every Effect produced by one TaskExecution commits atomically.

A committed MaterialMovement or placement transition may serve as the authoritative record of its Effect. A universal Effect table or event-sourced persistence model is not required.

### Partial and Uncertain Outcomes

TaskExecution lifecycle state and Effect commitment remain independent.

A failed or canceled TaskExecution may retain committed Effects. Failure does not automatically reverse accepted inventory changes.

When physical outcomes are uncertain:

- Known Events and evidence are preserved.
- Unverified outcomes remain explicitly unresolved.
- Missing acknowledgments are not treated as proof of non-occurrence.
- Unverified physical changes are not committed as confirmed inventory facts.
- Dependent operations are restricted where required.
- Physical commands are not blindly repeated.

Confirmed partial outcomes may be committed independently when permitted by the applicable contracts.

### Idempotency and Recovery

Each logical Effect has a stable identity across commitment retries.

Repeated submissions of the same Effect with identical content must not duplicate authoritative changes. Conflicting submissions using the same identity are rejected.

Software commitment retries do not imply that repeating a physical operation is safe.

Recovery uses additional evidence, authorized observations, or reconciliation to resolve uncertain outcomes.

When a physical discrepancy is established without a known cause, reconciliation may correct authoritative inventory state without inventing the missing historical activity.

Historical Events, accepted Effects, and supporting evidence are preserved. Corrections are represented through additional authorized Effects rather than destructive modification.

### Initial Scope

The initial implementation supports immutable operational Events, structured evidence, stable Effect identities, and traceable domain-specific commitments.

Evidence may be represented using PostgreSQL JSONB and references to external artifacts, subject to applicable validation and retention requirements.

A generic Event store, universal Effect table, distributed event bus, and automated recovery engine are deferred.

## Alternatives Considered

**Treat Events as authoritative inventory changes.** Rejected because observed or reported activity does not independently establish valid domain Effects.

**Treat TaskExecution completion as proof of commitment.** Rejected because execution lifecycle and authoritative state have separate responsibilities.

**Require every Effect to use a universal persistence model.** Rejected because domain-specific records can preserve authoritative state and commitment history without an additional generic abstraction.

**Automatically reverse Effects when Tasks fail.** Rejected because failure does not invalidate previously committed or physically completed outcomes.

**Treat software and physical retries identically.** Rejected because repeating an irreversible laboratory action may change physical reality a second time.

## Consequences

- Observations and reports remain distinguishable from authoritative state.
- Inventory changes require explicit domain validation and commitment.
- Failed, partial, and uncertain executions retain their established consequences.
- Repeated commitment requests cannot duplicate Effects.
- Recovery preserves historical integrity without fabricating physical outcomes.
- Domain-specific persistence remains possible without requiring full event sourcing.
- Evidence validation, correlation, and recovery introduce additional implementation responsibilities.
