# ADR-010: Correction, Reconciliation, and Historical Integrity

## Status

Proposed

## Context

Laboratory inventory records may be incorrect. An operator might record the wrong transfer quantity, scan the wrong Container, or register a PO incorrectly. Physical measurements may also reveal discrepancies between recorded inventory and observed reality.

Correcting an erroneous record is different from reconciling an unexplained physical discrepancy. Neither operation necessarily represents a new physical transfer.

Corrections must preserve historical accountability while respecting legitimate activity that occurred after the original record was committed.

We need explicit correction semantics that restore trustworthy authoritative state without rewriting history or inventing physical events.

## Decision

We preserve committed MaterialMovements and postings as immutable historical records. Corrections are represented through additional, authorized, and explicitly linked MaterialMovements.

We distinguish three correction operations.

### ReverseMovement

`ReverseMovement` compensates an erroneous committed MaterialMovement.

Each original posting is matched by a new posting with the opposite signed quantity, preserving a one-to-one correspondence with the original postings.

The reversal references the original movement and its postings, together with the required authorization and evidence.

A reversal corrects recorded history; it does not establish that the corresponding physical action was reversed.

The initial implementation permits at most one effective reversal of a MaterialMovement.

### ReplaceMovement

`ReplaceMovement` corrects an erroneous movement by combining its reversal with a replacement movement containing the accepted quantities and accountable positions.

The reversal and replacement commit as one validated atomic group.

The replacement explicitly supersedes the original movement. Both the original and corrective records remain traceable.

A replacement must preserve all applicable inventory invariants, including the validity of subsequent activity.

### ReconcileQuantity

`ReconcileQuantity` resolves a discrepancy between an observed physical quantity and the currently recorded quantity.

For a holding with recorded quantity \(Q_r\) and observed quantity \(Q_o\):

$$
\Delta q = Q_o-Q_r
$$

The adjustment is recorded through balanced postings between the affected ContainerContent and an appropriate nonphysical discrepancy position.

Reconciliation requires supporting observation evidence and authorization.

It changes the authoritative recorded quantity without asserting an unverified historical cause.

### Correction Integrity

All corrections are validated against current authoritative state.

We adopt the following rules:

- Original MaterialMovements, postings, Events, and supporting evidence are preserved.
- Corrective movements retain explicit references to the records they correct.
- Corrections cannot produce unauthorized negative physical balances.
- A correction must not silently erase, reinterpret, or invalidate legitimate subsequent activity.
- Correction groups commit atomically or are rejected without partial Effects.
- Repeated correction requests are idempotent, and conflicting requests using the same logical identity are rejected.
- Corrections require appropriate authority and evidence.

When a correction cannot preserve current invariants, it is rejected or requires explicit, evidence-backed resolution.

The same historical integrity principle applies to other authoritative records, including scientific assertions, quality decisions, and placement transitions. Their specific correction operations remain governed by their respective domain contracts.

## Alternatives Considered

**Modify or delete erroneous historical records.** Rejected because destructive changes would undermine auditability and historical reconstruction.

**Automatically reverse all downstream movements.** Rejected because subsequent activity may be legitimate and must not be silently invalidated.

**Use reconciliation for every inventory error.** Rejected because correcting a known erroneous record differs from recording a physical discrepancy whose cause is unknown.

**Allow unrestricted corrective postings.** Rejected because corrections must preserve the same quantity and authorization invariants as ordinary movements.

## Consequences

- Historical records remain reconstructable even after correction.
- Erroneous movements can be reversed or replaced without rewriting accepted history.
- Physical discrepancies can be reconciled without inventing their causes.
- Legitimate subsequent activity remains protected.
- Corrections require additional evidence, authorization, and concurrency validation.
- Some historical errors cannot be corrected directly when later activity prevents a valid compensating transition; explicit resolution is then required.
