# ADR-007: Quality Decisions, Expiration, and Operational Eligibility

## Status

Proposed

## Context

Recorded inventory does not necessarily represent Material that can be used.

A Material may fail QC, remain awaiting approval, expire, experience unsuitable storage conditions, or be subject to operational restrictions. These conditions can affect its usability without changing its recorded quantity.

Eligibility also depends on intended use. A Material suitable for one Task may not satisfy the requirements of another.

Scientific corrections may make previously accepted approvals stale through direct or transitive dependencies. Notifications can be delayed, missed, or processed incompletely; reassessment may fail or lack evidence. Eligibility correctness cannot depend on successful downstream propagation.

The inventory system must distinguish physical stock from operational eligibility while preserving decisions and evidence and validating their required scientific dependencies on demand.

## Decision

We derive **operational eligibility on demand from authoritative state, applicable Specifications and policies, and the complete transitive graph of scientific dependencies required for the intended use**, rather than storing usability as an intrinsic property of Material or relying on propagated approval status.

### Quality Decisions and Restrictions

Quality decisions and restrictions are explicit, authorized, and historically traceable.

They may apply to a Material, ContainerContent, or another defined scope.

Each decision retains its scope, outcome, authority, supporting evidence, effective history, governing versions, and required versioned dependencies under ADR-006.

We adopt the following rules:

- QC approval, rejection, and restrictions do not independently change recorded quantity.
- Decisions affecting a Material may restrict its holdings according to their declared scope.
- Holding-specific decisions do not automatically apply to other holdings of the same Material.
- Changes to decisions preserve previous outcomes and supporting evidence.
- Dependent Materials may require reassessment when relevant source decisions change, according to applicable Specifications.

### Expiration and Stability

Expiration is assessed using the applicable `MaterialSpecification`, provenance, holding history, storage conditions, and intended use.

We do not assume that every Material has one fixed expiration date.

An expiration assessment returns:

$$
\operatorname{ExpirationAssessment}(m,c,u,t,S,\Sigma)
\rightarrow
\{\mathrm{Valid},\mathrm{Expired},\mathrm{Unknown}\}
$$

Storage-dependent assessments use effective-dated placement history and required environmental evidence.

Aliquoting, dilution, and other transformations do not universally preserve or reset expiration. Their stability requirements follow the governing Specifications.

Missing evidence required to establish stability produces `Unknown`, rather than an assumption of validity.

### Operational Eligibility

Eligibility is evaluated for a Material or ContainerContent `m`, intended use `u`, assessment time `t`, relevant authoritative state `S`, and applicable published Specification versions and policies supplied by `Σ`:

$$
\operatorname{Eligible}(m,u,t,S,\Sigma)
\rightarrow
\{\mathrm{Yes},\mathrm{No},\mathrm{Unknown}\}
$$

- `Yes` indicates that the applicable requirements for the intended use are satisfied.
- `No` indicates that a known condition prevents the intended use.
- `Unknown` indicates that required information is insufficient to establish eligibility.

`Unknown` does not authorize use.

Evaluation must:

1. Resolve the applicable published Specification versions and governing policies using explicit bindings under ADR-002, not implicit latest-version selection.
2. Determine the scientific assertions, QC decisions, restrictions, provenance dependencies, and other evidence required for the intended use.
3. Traverse the complete transitive graph of required dependencies under ADR-006, without requiring unrelated provenance branches.
4. Validate each required dependency, its accepted source versions, and relevant corrections against current authoritative state and its applicable versioned contract. Required dependency completeness must itself be established.
5. Evaluate applicable holding-specific quality, expiration, containment, storage history, restrictions, quantity requirements, and other use conditions.
6. Return `Yes` only when every applicable requirement is established as satisfied. Return `No` when authoritative evidence or policy establishes prohibition; otherwise return `Unknown` when required validation is unresolved.

An assessment must use a consistent authoritative basis across its required dependencies, contracts, and evidence; combining individually successful checks from incompatible states cannot establish `Yes`.

A required dependency that is stale, superseded in a relevant way, missing, or awaiting reassessment cannot support `Yes`. Staleness alone is not a negative scientific conclusion: it yields `Unknown` unless authoritative evidence or policy establishes `No`. Historical approvals and their evidence remain preserved.

Validation must terminate or return an unresolved outcome. Cycles, malformed dependencies, missing references, unavailable evidence, or exhausted validation capacity cannot yield `Yes`; absent an established prohibition, they yield `Unknown`. No artificial maximum provenance depth defines scientific validity. Shared dependencies may be assessed once per consistent assessment. Caching, incremental computation, or precomputed information may be used only with established completeness, freshness, and validity for the current assessment; no result depends on a particular reassessment scheduling strategy.

### Reassessment Requests and Retry

On-demand evaluation identifies dependencies requiring reassessment where their applicability can be established and, when supported, requests reassessment of the necessary dependencies. Requests have an idempotent identity or equivalent deduplicated correlation, so repeated discoveries by multiple clients do not create unlimited duplicate work for the same unresolved basis. A newly changed dependency basis must remain distinguishable from an earlier request.

A reassessment request, scheduling acknowledgment, and accepted scientific conclusion are distinct. Requesting or scheduling reassessment does not itself establish an authoritative scientific Effect or resolve eligibility. A revised conclusion requires the normal evidence, authority, and Effect commitment under ADR-009. If requesting, scheduling, or completing reassessment fails, eligibility still returns `Unknown` or `No` as appropriate.

Clients receiving `Unknown` may reevaluate after relevant evidence, decisions, or reassessment results become available. Each retry must perform or rely on a fresh, valid authoritative assessment; `Unknown` never automatically becomes `Yes`. The logical response may expose unresolved dependency references or reassessment status to guide reevaluation, without making clients responsible for scientific validity or requiring tight polling.

Correctness belongs to on-demand validation; when and how reassessment executes is a separate future policy. No immediate recursive refresh, FIFO processing, or event-driven propagation is assumed. Fair or priority scheduling, dependency-aware ordering, batching and deduplication, bounded concurrency and backpressure, work coalescing, and cost-aware optimization are illustrative future alternatives, not commitments. A request guarantees neither immediate nor successful reassessment.

### Admission, Continuation, and Authorization

A read-only eligibility result describes an assessment at a particular authoritative state; it is not an indefinite authorization, reservation, or guarantee that later use remains permitted.

Workflow admission and relevant TaskExecution continuation checkpoints under ADR-008 must validate required dependencies and other use conditions against sufficiently current authoritative state at their authorization boundaries. Admission retains the input identities, intended use, governing versions, and supporting assertion, decision, and evidence references. Earlier admission does not unconditionally authorize subsequent affected operations.

An authorization must not commit using a required dependency made stale by a correction that has already committed. If a source correction commits after a dependency was checked but before authorization, the boundary must reject or revalidate the assessment under the corrected state. This guarantee applies to relevant changes in dependency relationships and governing bindings as well as source assertions, and follows ADR-011 without selecting an enforcement mechanism. Assessment reuse must establish that its required basis remains valid at this boundary.

From the relevant correction's commitment, missing notifications or unfinished reassessment cannot create a stale-approval window. A subsequent affected operation may begin only after satisfying continuation conditions or an explicit intervention decision permitted by the governing Specifications and appropriate authority; non-waivable requirements cannot be overridden.

Physical work already authorized and initiated is not retroactively undone. Confirmed, partial, or uncertain outcomes remain subject to ADR-008–009, and confirmed Effects remain recordable after a scientific correction. A prechecked but not yet initiated operation must pass its applicable initiation boundary under current state. No database transaction is assumed to span physical execution, and neither scientific reassessment nor a changed eligibility result automatically reverses quantity postings or historical authorizations.

### Available Quantity

Available quantity is derived from recorded physical inventory and eligibility for the intended use.

For an active physical holding with nonnegative recorded quantity:

$$
0\leq Q_{\mathrm{available}}(a,u,t)\leq Q_{\mathrm{recorded}}(a)
$$

A holding with eligibility `No` or `Unknown` contributes zero authorized available quantity for that use.

Applicable restrictions may reduce availability further.

Availability is not maintained as an independent accounting balance. Expiration, QC decisions, and restrictions do not create MaterialMovements or dispose of physical stock.

### Initial Scope

The initial model supports scoped QC decisions, explicit stability limits, storage-dependent expiration assessment, on-demand dependency-aware eligibility, and idempotently identifiable reassessment requests where supported. Request-processing availability is not a precondition for a safe eligibility result.

Automated scientific reassessment, scheduling and processing policies, comprehensive environmental monitoring, and generalized eligibility-policy interpretation are deferred. No generic dependency engine or scheduling platform is required.

## Alternatives Considered

**Store a mutable `is_usable` flag on Material.** Rejected because eligibility depends on intended use, holding state, time, and applicable Specifications.

**Treat expiration or QC rejection as consumption.** Rejected because unusable Material may remain physically present and accountable.

**Assign one universal expiration date to each Material.** Rejected because stability may depend on preparation, handling, storage history, and intended use.

**Treat missing evidence as approval.** Rejected because unresolved requirements cannot establish eligibility.

**Trust propagated approval status or successful reassessment scheduling.** Rejected because notifications and processing can fail or lag behind current scientific dependencies.

**Invalidate every provenance descendant.** Rejected because required dependencies are use- and Specification-relative.

**Treat stale approval as scientific rejection.** Rejected because required reassessment may reaffirm the conclusion; unresolved validity is `Unknown`, not automatically `No`.

## Consequences

- Recorded and available inventory remain distinct.
- QC decisions and restrictions can change usability without modifying quantities.
- Eligibility is evaluated for a specific intended use and time.
- Historical decisions, stability assertions, and supporting evidence remain traceable.
- Storage history and scientific provenance can influence stability assessments.
- Availability queries require complete applicable dependency validation, not successful propagation Event processing.
- On-demand evaluation and authorization-time freshness checks introduce work; valid reuse may reduce it without weakening safety.
- Reassessment requests and processing may be unavailable without permitting stale use; historical approvals and physical outcomes remain intact.

### Acceptance Criteria

- **A — Direct correction:** M2's approval depends on A1. After a correction to A1 requiring reassessment, M2 returns `Unknown` or an authoritatively established `No`, never `Yes` solely from its previous approval.
- **B — Transitive correction:** M3 depends on an assertion about M2 that depends on A1. Evaluation detects a relevant A1 correction without any downstream propagation Event being processed.
- **C — Failed notification or worker:** Lost or delayed notifications and failed reassessment processing do not prevent authoritative dependency validation from detecting staleness.
- **D — Concurrent authorization:** A correction committed after evaluation starts but before authorization prevents commitment based on the stale assessment; the boundary rejects or revalidates it. Already initiated physical outcomes remain recordable.
- **E — Duplicate requests:** Multiple clients discovering the same stale dependency produce idempotently correlated or deduplicated reassessment requests, without changing eligibility or treating scheduling as a scientific conclusion.
- **F — Missing evidence:** A required dependency or its evidence cannot be resolved. The result is `Unknown` absent an independently established prohibition, never assumed validity.
- **G — Irrelevant provenance:** An ancestor's unrelated correction does not invalidate eligibility when that branch is not required for the intended use and all applicable requirements are satisfied.
- **H — Historical integrity:** Correction and reassessment preserve prior scientific assertions, dependencies, QC approvals, authorization decisions, execution history, and committed quantity Effects.
- **Incomplete validation:** Cycles, malformed relationships, missing references, and inability to finish required traversal terminate safely with an unresolved result, not `Yes`; stale caches and retries cannot bypass validation.
