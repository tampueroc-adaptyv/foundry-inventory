# ADR-002: Specification Versioning and Binding

## Status

Proposed

## Context

Materials, Containers, and Locations are governed by Specifications defining their scientific identity, physical capabilities, compatibility, and operational constraints.

These definitions may evolve as laboratory methods change or new material types are introduced. However, changing a Specification must not silently reinterpret existing Entities, historical decisions, or TaskExecutions.

We need a versioning and binding model that preserves historical reproducibility while allowing Specifications to evolve independently of the Entities they govern.

## Decision

Specifications have stable identities and immutable published versions. Entities bind explicitly to an applicable Specification version.

We adopt the following rules:

- **Immutable versions:** Published Specification versions cannot be modified. Multiple versions may coexist.
- **Explicit binding:** Material, Container, and Location Entities are assigned an applicable Specification version at creation.
- **Historical consistency:** Publishing a new version does not automatically change existing bindings or reinterpret historical operations.
- **Authorized rebinding:** An Entity may transition to another applicable Specification version through an explicit, authorized Effect that preserves its identity and previous binding history.
- **Applicability:** The responsible domain validates that a Specification version is applicable to the Entity and intended operation. Publication alone does not establish applicability.
- **Reassessment:** Changes to Specifications or bindings trigger reassessment only according to the applicable versioned policies and dependencies.

TaskExecutions retain the Specification versions governing their validated inputs, requirements, and Effects. Subsequent changes do not retroactively alter those interpretations.

The initial implementation resolves current bindings from explicit assignment history. It does not implicitly select the latest published version or automatically migrate existing Entities.

Specifications may define different structures and validation rules without requiring separate persistence models for every material type.

## Alternatives Considered

**Mutable Specifications.** Rejected because changing definitions in place would undermine historical reproducibility and traceability.

**Automatically applying the latest version.** Rejected because publication does not establish applicability or authorize changes to existing Entities.

**Permanently fixed bindings.** Rejected because legitimate scientific reassessment and changes in governing requirements may require an Entity to adopt another Specification version without changing its identity.

## Consequences

- Existing Entities and historical operations retain their governing Specification versions.
- New material types and evolving laboratory requirements can be introduced without redefining the core identity model.
- Rebinding and reassessment require explicit validation and auditable history.
- Version selection and compatibility introduce additional responsibility for domain operations.
- Generic Specification interpreters, automatic migration, and detailed reassessment workflows are deferred.
