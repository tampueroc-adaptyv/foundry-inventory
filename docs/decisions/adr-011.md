# ADR-011: Transaction Boundaries, Concurrency, and Idempotency

## Status

Proposed

## Context

Laboratory inventory is modified by concurrent Tasks, human operators, instruments, and external systems.

Multiple TaskExecutions may consume Material from the same ContainerContent, relocate Containers, or propose conflicting Effects. Requests may be retried after timeouts, leaving their commitment outcomes uncertain.

The system must preserve authoritative domain invariants despite concurrency and failures. Database transactions can protect accepted state changes, but cannot make physical laboratory activity atomic or reversible.

We need explicit commitment boundaries and retry semantics without requiring distributed transactions or a generalized coordination framework.

## Decision

We commit authoritative Effects through **bounded PostgreSQL transactions**, with validation against protected current state.

### Transaction Boundaries

Each authoritative domain operation defines the Effects that must commit atomically and the invariants governing their acceptance.

We adopt the following rules:

- An atomic Effect group is fully committed or rejected without partial authoritative changes.
- All postings of a `MaterialMovement` commit within one transaction.
- Entity creation and its required initial Specification binding commit together.
- Placement transitions atomically replace the affected authoritative placement while preserving historical records.
- Corrective movement groups preserve the atomicity required by ADR-010.
- Validation and commitment occur against the authoritative state protected by the transaction, not solely against earlier observations or eligibility assessments.

A `TaskExecution` may produce multiple independently committed Effects. Its lifecycle does not establish a transaction boundary for all its consequences.

Physical Tasks do not hold database transactions open throughout their execution.

### Concurrency Control

Concurrent operations must not violate the invariants established by their responsible domains.

The initial implementation uses PostgreSQL transactional constraints and explicit locking where operations affect shared authoritative state.

- Quantity-changing operations protect the affected accountable positions and validate resulting balances before commitment.
- Placement transitions protect affected Containers, parents, and relevant occupancy constraints.
- Specification rebindings validate the expected current assignment before accepting a successor.
- Lifecycle transitions validate expected current states and applicable preconditions.
- Operations acquiring multiple locks use a deterministic ordering to reduce deadlock risk.

Database constraints enforce structural guarantees where possible. Domain validation enforces invariants that require evaluation across multiple Entities, Specifications, or historical records.

Concurrent conflicts are rejected or retried according to the applicable operation contract. A previously successful validation does not bypass commitment-time checks.

### Idempotency

Each logical Effect has a stable identity independent of individual submission attempts.

We adopt the following rules:

- An identical retry of an accepted Effect resolves to the existing commitment without applying it again.
- Reusing an Effect identity with a conflicting payload is rejected.
- The Effect identity and its committed outcome remain recoverable after a client timeout.
- Atomic Effect groups retain stable logical identities and constituent references across retries.
- Authorized corrections use distinct Effect identities linked to the original commitments.

Idempotency protects authoritative state commitment, not physical execution.

Repeating a robot command or manual laboratory action requires separate authorization under the applicable TaskSchema and available evidence.

### Cross-Boundary Coordination

Effects that cannot be committed within one supported transaction boundary are coordinated through explicit dependencies and TaskExecution state.

A dependent operation proceeds only after its required preceding outcome has been established.

Failure of a later operation does not automatically reverse earlier committed Effects.

Partial and uncertain outcomes remain traceable and follow the recovery rules established by ADR-008–010.

The initial implementation does not require distributed transactions, global locks, or a generalized workflow coordinator.

### Initial Scope

PostgreSQL is the authoritative persistence boundary for inventory state.

The implementation uses short-lived transactions, database constraints, and targeted concurrency control for quantity movements, placement transitions, and other supported inventory Effects.

Transactionally maintained projections may support efficient reads, provided they remain consistent with and reconcilable against authoritative history.

Detailed physical resource coordination, distributed commitment, and automated recovery remain outside the initial scope.

## Alternatives Considered

**Application-level validation without transactional protection.** Rejected because concurrent changes could invalidate previously checked preconditions.

**Optimistic validation without database constraints.** Rejected as the sole protection mechanism because shared inventory invariants require authoritative enforcement. Optimistic concurrency may still be used where appropriate.

**One transaction for an entire TaskExecution.** Rejected because physical execution may be lengthy, partial, or uncertain.

**Distributed transactions across operational systems.** Rejected because the initial inventory implementation does not require global atomicity across independent authorities.

**Treating repeated physical commands as idempotent.** Rejected because repeating laboratory activity may produce additional physical consequences.

## Consequences

- Concurrent inventory operations cannot legitimately violate protected domain invariants.
- Atomic Effect groups commit completely or not at all.
- Duplicate commitment requests do not duplicate authoritative changes.
- Client timeouts do not require assuming that a commitment failed.
- Physical activity remains independent of database transaction boundaries.
- Partial and uncertain Task outcomes remain representable.
- Locking, contention, and transaction retries introduce implementation complexity that must be covered by integration tests.
