# ADR-001: Scientific Material Identity

## Status

Proposed

## Context

Laboratory Tasks receive, produce, divide, and transform Materials. These activities may preserve an existing scientific identity or establish a new one.

Scientific identity is independent of physical containment. The same Material may occupy multiple Containers, while independently produced preparations may have identical scientific attributes but require distinct identities.

The Material domain defines identity relative to a `MaterialSpecification`. Matching attributes alone does not establish identity, and physical movement alone does not create a new Material.

Laboratory preparations may also progress through intermediate states before a final preparation is established. These intermediates may represent physically existing Material even when the intended final output has not yet been produced.

We need an identity policy that supports traceability without imposing universal identity rules across all material types and scientific transformations.

## Decision

A `Material` is a scientific Entity with stable identity, governed by an explicit `MaterialSpecification` version.

We adopt **distinct preparation identity by default**, subject to the applicable MaterialSpecification:

- Independently produced or sourced preparations establish distinct Materials by default, even when their scientific attributes match. Additional receipts of the same verified preparation do not independently require a new identity.
- Physical transfers, relocation, and aliquoting preserve Material identity unless the applicable Specification establishes a different identity boundary.
- Scientific transformations, including dilution and composite preparation, require an `IdentityDecision` under the applicable MaterialSpecification.
- Material identity is not inferred solely from descriptive attributes, Container identity, or physical location.

An `IdentityDecision` determines whether a proposed output reuses an existing Material or establishes a distinct one:

$$
\operatorname{IdentityDecision}(\Sigma,o)
\rightarrow
\{\operatorname{Reuse}(M),\operatorname{Create}(M')\}
$$

Identity validation follows the applicable Specification and may require scientific evidence or authorized judgment.

### Intermediate and Final Preparations

Intermediate preparations may be represented as distinct Materials when they establish independently identifiable scientific preparations under the applicable MaterialSpecification.

An intermediate Material retains its identity and history independently of whether the intended final preparation is subsequently completed.

When a Task establishes a final preparation, its output undergoes an `IdentityDecision`. If a distinct final Material is created, its derivation from the intermediate Material is preserved.

Establishing the final Material does not overwrite or replace the intermediate Material's historical identity. Any associated quantity changes are accounted for separately through MaterialMovements.

Preparation completeness and operational eligibility are not intrinsic properties of Material identity. They are established through applicable Specifications, decisions, and authoritative Effects.

Distinct Materials may be considered scientifically equivalent for a defined purpose without merging their identities or histories.

Scientific provenance and composition remain separate from identity. Derivation relationships do not independently establish equivalence or available inventory.

## Alternatives Considered

**Identity based on matching scientific attributes.** Rejected because independent preparations may share properties while having different origins and histories.

**A new Material for each physical holding.** Rejected because physical containment and scientific identity are independent.

**Universal identity preservation or creation for derived outputs.** Rejected because identity boundaries are Specification-dependent.

**Represent intermediate preparations only as TaskExecution state.** Rejected because physically existing intermediate Materials may require independent scientific identity, provenance, and inventory accounting even when their generating TaskExecution remains incomplete.

## Consequences

- Material identity remains stable across ordinary physical inventory operations.
- Independently produced preparations retain distinct identities and traceable histories.
- Intermediate preparations can be independently identified and accounted for before final preparations are established.
- Finalization may establish a distinct Material without erasing the intermediate Material's history.
- Identity decisions require Specification-governed validation.
- Scientific equivalence does not merge identities or erase history.
- Specification binding, physical containment, provenance, quantity accounting, and preparation eligibility are addressed in subsequent ADRs.
