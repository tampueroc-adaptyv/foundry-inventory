# ADR-008: TaskSchema and TaskExecution

## Status

Proposed

## Context

Laboratory work involves Tasks such as receiving Material, aliquoting preparations, transferring liquids between plate wells, and producing composite preparations.

Each Task has requirements governing its inputs, outputs, resources, and permitted changes to authoritative state. Actual executions may succeed, fail, partially complete, or remain uncertain.

Complex laboratory processes may require multiple Tasks with dependencies between them. For example, preparing a pooled Material may involve creating an intermediate preparation, transferring contributions from several wells, and finalizing the preparation once required conditions are satisfied.

A Task's execution status does not necessarily describe its physical consequences or establish which inventory changes have been committed.

We need to distinguish individual Tasks from their composition into workflows, without requiring a generalized workflow engine.

## Decision

We represent laboratory work through versioned `TaskSchemas`, identifiable `TaskExecutions`, and explicit workflow composition where required.

### TaskSchema

A `TaskSchema` defines a versioned contract for a kind of laboratory Task, including the identity and requirements of its inputs and outputs.

Task boundaries are determined by meaningful operational contracts and authoritative Effects, not necessarily by individual instrument commands or high-level workflow stages.

Each published TaskSchema version declares:

- Typed input and output ports, including their identity and requirements.
- Resource requirements and operational preconditions.
- Permitted Effect families.
- Execution lifecycle and allowed transitions.
- Required checkpoints.
- Rules governing when Effects may be proposed.
- Conditions required for successful completion.

TaskSchemas may reference applicable Material, Container, and Location Specifications.

Published versions are immutable. Existing TaskExecutions retain their governing TaskSchema version when new versions are introduced.

### TaskExecution

A `TaskExecution` represents an identifiable invocation of a TaskSchema.

Each execution retains:

- Its governing TaskSchema version and applicable Specification bindings.
- Actual inputs, outputs, and resource bindings.
- Responsible actors and external execution references.
- Lifecycle transitions and checkpoint outcomes.
- Relevant Events and supporting evidence.
- Proposed, committed, rejected, or unresolved Effect references.

A TaskExecution has a stable identity independent of its lifecycle state.

TaskExecutions may exist independently of WorkflowExecutions. One TaskExecution may produce zero, one, or multiple Effects.

### Workflow Composition

A `WorkflowSchema` defines a composition of TaskSchemas through dependencies and typed input/output connections. A `WorkflowExecution` represents an invocation of that composition.

A WorkflowExecution coordinates TaskExecutions without replacing their independent lifecycles or domain authority.

We adopt the following rules:

- TaskExecutions may be composed through explicit dependencies, including required Task completion, checkpoint satisfaction, or committed Effects.
- A WorkflowExecution may contain multiple invocations of the same TaskSchema, each with distinct input bindings.
- Workflow coordination does not imply atomic execution or commitment across all participating Tasks.
- Confirmed Effects remain authoritative even when a dependent Task or the overall WorkflowExecution fails.
- Workflow progress does not independently establish Material identity, quantity, provenance, or eligibility.

For example, a pooled preparation may be established through Tasks that create an intermediate Material, transfer confirmed contributions, and finalize the preparation.

Each transfer TaskExecution retains its own outcome and Effects. Finalization depends on the required contributions, scientific assertions, and evidence being established.

Task granularity follows meaningful execution and recovery boundaries. A physical instrument command may affect multiple wells and does not necessarily correspond to one TaskExecution per well.

The initial implementation supports explicit Task dependencies and bounded workflow composition without a generalized orchestration engine.

### Lifecycle and Checkpoints

Each TaskSchema defines or references a mandatory lifecycle contract.

The initial lifecycle supports:

- `Pending`
- `Running`
- `AwaitingEvidence`
- `Completed`
- `Failed`
- `Canceled`

Transitions follow the governing TaskSchema and its required checkpoints.

Checkpoints may validate input bindings, Material eligibility, resource compatibility, physical outcomes, and required Effect commitments.

A TaskExecution cannot complete until its required completion conditions are satisfied.

Failure, cancellation, or uncertainty does not imply that no physical activity occurred or automatically reverse previously committed Effects.

Checkpoints may be reevaluated when relevant evidence or authoritative state changes. Event-driven notifications or polling may initiate reevaluation, but neither independently satisfies a checkpoint or commits an Effect.

### Operational Resources

TaskSchemas declare the physical resources and access requirements needed for execution.

Resource bindings may reference Containers, individual wells, ContainerContents, Locations, instruments, or other operational resources.

Access requirements depend on the Task and applicable resource Specifications. Exclusive access to an entire Container is not universally required.

Resource bindings do not independently establish physical custody, exclusive access, or Material allocation.

Where physical resource coordination is required, incompatible access must be prevented before the relevant operation proceeds.

The initial implementation does not include a distributed scheduler or generalized resource-claim mechanism. Execution concurrency requiring unsupported physical coordination remains outside the initial operational scope.

### Effects and Domain Authority

TaskSchemas declare which Effect families their executions may propose.

Permission to propose an Effect is distinct from authority to commit it.

The responsible domain validates proposed Effects against its current authoritative state, applicable Specifications, evidence, authorization, and invariants.

TaskExecution completion does not substitute for successful Effect commitment.

Confirmed partial outcomes remain representable even when the overall TaskExecution or WorkflowExecution fails.

The initial implementation supports a bounded set of inventory TaskSchemas and explicit dependencies between their executions.

Generalized workflow orchestration, instrument control, scheduling, reservations, distributed resource coordination, and automated recovery are deferred.

## Alternatives Considered

**Represent Tasks solely through inventory mutations.** Rejected because execution history, partial outcomes, and uncertainty would be difficult to distinguish from accepted state changes.

**Use mutable Task definitions.** Rejected because historical executions could be reinterpreted when their governing contracts change.

**Treat Task completion as proof of inventory changes.** Rejected because execution lifecycle and authoritative Effect commitment have distinct responsibilities.

**Represent an entire laboratory workflow as one TaskExecution.** Rejected as a universal approach because independently meaningful Tasks may have distinct inputs, outputs, dependencies, and partial outcomes.

**Require one TaskExecution per instrument command or well.** Rejected because physical command granularity does not universally determine Task boundaries.

**Implement a generalized workflow engine.** Rejected for the initial scope because explicit Task dependencies are sufficient for the selected inventory workflows.

## Consequences

- Intended work, individual executions, and workflow composition remain independently identifiable.
- Historical TaskExecutions retain their governing contracts and relevant evidence.
- Intermediate preparations can be produced and accumulated through multiple TaskExecutions before finalization.
- Partial, failed, and uncertain executions remain representable without losing confirmed inventory changes.
- Workflow dependencies can reference committed domain Effects rather than relying solely on Task completion.
- Operational resource requirements remain distinct from inventory quantity and placement constraints.
- Authoritative domains retain responsibility for validating and committing Effects.
- Explicit workflow dependencies introduce coordination complexity without requiring a complete workflow engine.
