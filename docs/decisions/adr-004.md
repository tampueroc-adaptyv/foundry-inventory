# ADR-004: Placement and Location Semantics

## Status

Proposed

## Context

Containers move between physical places throughout laboratory operations. Tubes may be stored in boxes, boxes in freezers, and plates inside instruments. Some Containers, such as plate wells, have fixed positions within their parent Containers.

The system must identify where a Container is currently located and reconstruct its placement history. Placement also determines the storage environment experienced by its contents, which may affect Material stability and eligibility.

Physical relocation is distinct from transferring Material between Containers. Moving a tube does not change its Material identity or recorded quantity.

We need a consistent representation of physical containment, positions, and movement without conflating Containers with Locations.

## Decision

We distinguish `Location`, representing a physical place or address, from `Container`, representing a physical object capable of holding Material or other Containers.

Locations may form a hierarchy, such as laboratory, room, freezer, and shelf. Containers may be placed within Locations or other Containers.

Physical placement is represented as a relationship between a child Container and its enclosing Container or Location, with an optional position.

We adopt the following rules:

- **Unique placement:** A Container has at most one active physical placement at a time.
- **Acyclic containment:** Container and Location hierarchies must not contain cycles.
- **Position constraints:** Placement must satisfy the applicable `ContainerSpecification` and `LocationSpecification`, including compatibility, occupancy, and capacity.
- **Fixed positions:** Containers such as plate wells retain their specified parent and position. Movable Containers may change placement through authorized transitions.
- **Historical traceability:** Accepted placement transitions preserve previous placements, actors, timestamps, and supporting evidence.
- **Independent quantity:** Relocation does not create a `MaterialMovement` or change the recorded quantities of the Container's contents.

Each Location binds to a versioned `LocationSpecification` defining its applicable storage conditions, containment requirements, capabilities, and constraints.

A Container's effective storage condition is determined from its enclosing placement chain, using the nearest Location that declares the relevant condition, according to the governing Specification.

Placement history retains the effective periods needed to assess Material exposure over time. A declared storage condition does not by itself prove that the actual environment satisfied it; required condition evidence is evaluated separately.

Changes to placement or relevant LocationSpecifications may require reassessment of Material eligibility without independently changing recorded inventory quantity.

The initial implementation supports current placement and effective-dated placement history. Full bitemporal reconstruction and automated environmental monitoring are deferred.

## Alternatives Considered

**Store a Location directly on each Material or ContainerContent.** Rejected because Material may occupy multiple Containers, and nested physical placement would require redundant location updates.

**Represent Containers and Locations as the same Entity type.** Rejected because movable physical objects and physical places have different identities, capabilities, and lifecycle constraints.

**Store only current placement.** Rejected because historical location and storage exposure are necessary for traceability and stability assessment.

**Represent relocation as MaterialMovement.** Rejected because relocating a Container does not transfer Material between accountable holdings.

## Consequences

- Container locations can be resolved through their physical placement hierarchy.
- Nested storage structures and fixed plate-well positions are supported.
- Relocation preserves Material, Container, and ContainerContent identities and quantities.
- Historical placements support traceability and storage-dependent stability assessments.
- Placement transitions require compatibility, exclusivity, and cycle validation.
- Environmental eligibility is evaluated separately from placement and quantity accounting.
