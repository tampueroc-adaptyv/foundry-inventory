# ADR-005: Quantity Accounting and MaterialMovement

## Status

Proposed

## Context

Laboratory Tasks receive, transfer, consume, produce, and dispose of Materials. Each activity may change the quantity recorded in one or more ContainerContents.

Materials are accounted for in different measurement dimensions, such as volume, mass, or count. Scientific properties such as concentration may influence how Materials are used but do not independently represent spendable inventory.

Quantity changes must remain correct under concurrent activity, repeated requests, failed Tasks, and corrections to erroneous records.

We need an accounting model that establishes trustworthy recorded quantities without conflating physical inventory, scientific composition, and operational eligibility.

## Decision

We adopt **immutable, balanced MaterialMovements** as the authoritative source of recorded quantities.

### Accountable Positions

A `ContainerContent` is the ordinary physical accountable position.

Nonphysical accountable positions represent external receipts, production, consumption, disposal, and discrepancies. These provide accounting counterparts without representing available physical inventory.

Each ContainerContent has one authoritative accounting dimension, established under its applicable `MaterialSpecification`. The initial implementation supports volume, mass, and count.

Compatible units are normalized using explicit conversion rules. Cross-dimensional conversions require separate scientific calculations and evidence.

### MaterialMovements

A `MaterialMovement` represents an accepted quantity-changing Effect containing uniquely identifiable postings.

Each posting identifies an accountable position, Material, measurement dimension, and signed quantity.

Every committed MaterialMovement must balance independently for each Material and measurement dimension:

$$
\sum_{i\in I_{m,d}}\Delta q_i=0
$$

All postings belonging to a MaterialMovement commit atomically. A movement is rejected if its postings are incomplete, incompatible, unbalanced, or would violate the resulting inventory constraints.

Recorded quantity derives exclusively from committed postings:

$$
Q_{\mathrm{recorded}}(a)=
\sum_{\text{committed postings at }a}\Delta q_i
$$

We adopt a **no-negative-active-holdings policy**: every active physical ContainerContent must have a recorded balance greater than or equal to zero after each committed movement or atomic Effect group. Any commitment that would leave an active physical holding negative is rejected, including transfers, consumption, disposal, corrections, and reconciliation. Concurrent commitments must preserve this invariant.

Nonphysical accountable positions may carry signed balances according to their declared accounting semantics; they do not represent available physical inventory.

A transactionally maintained balance projection may be used for performance, provided it remains reconcilable with the committed ledger.

### Quantity-Changing Operations

- **Receiving:** Credits a physical holding and debits an external receipt position.
- **Transfer:** Debits a source holding and credits a destination holding while preserving Material identity.
- **Consumption and disposal:** Transfer quantity from physical holdings to their respective nonphysical accounting positions.
- **Production:** Accounts for consumed inputs and produced outputs through independently balanced Material/dimension scopes. Scientific transformation may establish a new Material identity, provenance, and composition, but output quantity is not universally required to equal the sum of input quantities.

A physical relocation does not create a MaterialMovement.

Scientific composition and concentration do not create additional independently available inventory balances.

### Corrections and Commitment

Committed MaterialMovements and postings are immutable.

Erroneous movements are corrected through linked compensating movements. Physical discrepancies are recorded through authorized reconciliation supported by observation evidence.

Corrections must preserve current inventory invariants and legitimate subsequent activity. Reversing an erroneous record does not imply that a physical operation was reversed.

Each logical movement has a stable commitment identity. Repeated identical submissions must not duplicate quantity changes, while conflicting submissions using the same identity are rejected.

Concurrent commitments must not overdraw physical holdings. Validation and commitment occur against protected authoritative state.

### Recorded Versus Available Quantity

Recorded quantity represents accountable inventory, not necessarily material authorized for use.

Available quantity is derived separately from recorded quantity, intended use, eligibility, restrictions, and applicable Specifications.

Expiration or QC restrictions may reduce available quantity without changing recorded quantity. Availability is not maintained as an independent accounting balance.

## Alternatives Considered

**Directly mutable quantity fields.** Rejected as the authoritative model because quantity changes would not retain an independently reconstructable history.

**Single-sided inventory adjustments.** Rejected because they weaken the accounting guarantees provided by balanced postings.

**Treat scientific composition as inventory quantity.** Rejected because component contributions must not create duplicate spendable balances.

**Destructive correction of movements.** Rejected because it would erase previously accepted history and undermine traceability.

## Consequences

- Recorded quantities can be reconstructed from immutable movement history.
- Transfers, consumption, production, and disposal share consistent accounting semantics.
- Concurrent operations and retries require protected, idempotent commitment.
- Corrections preserve original records while updating authoritative balances.
- Scientific assertions, composition, and operational eligibility remain independent of quantity accounting.
- Concrete transaction mechanisms, correction workflows, and execution evidence are addressed by subsequent decisions.
