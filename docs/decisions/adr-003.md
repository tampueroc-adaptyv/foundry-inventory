# ADR-003: Container and ContainerContent Identity

## Status

Proposed

## Context

Materials occupy physical Containers such as tubes, vials, and plate wells. Containers may also hold other Containers, as with plates, boxes, and racks.

Scientific Material identity, physical Container identity, and the quantity held within a Container have different lifecycles.

A Container may be relocated, emptied, cleaned, or reused without becoming a new physical object. Likewise, the same Material may occupy multiple Containers, each with independently accountable quantities.

We need to represent these distinctions without conflating scientific identity, physical containment, and inventory accounting.

## Decision

We adopt separate identities for `Container` and `ContainerContent`, governed by their applicable Specifications.

**Container** represents an identifiable physical Entity capable of holding Material, other Containers, or both.

- Each Container has a stable identity independent of its contents and location.
- A Container binds to an explicit `ContainerSpecification` version defining its physical capabilities, compatibility, positions, and lifecycle constraints.
- Tubes, plate wells, plates, boxes, and racks share the Container abstraction, with their differences expressed through Specifications.
- A Container may be reused where permitted by its Specification. Retirement requires resolution of active contents and child placements.

**ContainerContent** represents an identifiable holding of a Material within a Container.

- Each ContainerContent references one Material and one direct material-holding Container.
- A Material may occupy multiple ContainerContents, each with independent quantity accounting and history.
- At most one active ContainerContent exists for the same Material within a single direct material-holding Container.
- Additional quantities of the same Material entering a compatible active holding contribute to that holding rather than creating parallel balances.
- Different Materials may coexist within a Container only where permitted by its Specification and applicable accounting rules.
- A ContainerContent retains its identity through quantity changes and relocation of its enclosing Container.
- Closing a ContainerContent requires zero recorded quantity and resolution of applicable physical outcomes. Zero quantity alone does not imply closure.

Physical identification is independent of Container identity. Barcodes and labels resolve to Containers without defining their identity, and historical identifier assignments remain traceable.

Container reuse does not reactivate closed ContainerContents or erase previous occupancy history.

## Alternatives Considered

**Treat Containers and their contents as one Entity.** Rejected because Containers and Material holdings have independent identities and lifecycles.

**Create a new Container for every change of contents.** Rejected because physical objects may be emptied and reused without losing their identity.

**Track quantities directly on Material.** Rejected because one Material may occupy multiple physical holdings with independently changing quantities.

**Model each Container type separately.** Rejected because tubes, wells, plates, and racks share containment semantics, while their capabilities and constraints vary by Specification.

## Consequences

- Scientific identity, physical containment, and accountable holdings remain independently traceable.
- Aliquoting can create multiple ContainerContents without necessarily creating new Materials.
- Plates, wells, boxes, and racks can share a consistent physical model.
- Reusable Containers preserve identity and historical occupancy.
- Compatibility, occupancy, and lifecycle constraints require explicit validation.
- Placement topology and history are addressed by ADR-004; quantity movements by ADR-005.
